## Overview

One question decides the rest: **where does the customer's identity live?**

| Where identity lives | What happens | What you build |
|---------------------|--------------|----------------|
| **With the shop** | Customers register and sign in with us, using an email and password or a social provider | Nothing |
| **With you, handed over per session** | Your site knows who the customer is and tells us, so they arrive already signed in | One API call |
| **With you, permanently** | The shop holds no credentials and sends customers to your provider to authenticate | Endpoints you host |

The last two combine: delegate to your provider for full-page visits, and hand us the identity directly for embedded ones.

Guest checkout is separate — see [Guest Checkout](#guest-checkout-works-alongside-any-of-these) below.

## Identity Lives With the Shop

Customers register and sign in with us: an email and password, plus whichever social providers the company has enabled. There is nothing for your integration to do.

This is the right answer when your site has no account system of its own, or when the two are separate concerns. It also carries the least maintenance: password resets, verification, social providers and account recovery are all handled.

See [Customer Accounts](/core-platform/ticket-shop/customer-accounts) for what a customer account holds.

## Identity Lives With You, Handed Over Per Session

Your site already knows who is signed in. Before rendering a frame or a link, your server tells us who the customer is and gets back a credential that signs them into the shop.

This is a single API call and no redirect, which makes it the natural fit for an embedded shop behind your own login — the frame renders with the customer already signed in.

You are asserting the identity, so you are responsible for having authenticated them. See [Pre-authenticating a Session](/integrate/identity/pre-authentication).

## Identity Lives With You, Permanently

The shop stops offering its own credentials. When a customer needs to sign in, the shop sends them to your identity provider; you authenticate them and hand them back. Password changes, email changes and sign-out route through you.

Choose this when you are the identity authority for these customers and a second set of credentials would not be acceptable — a membership organisation, a season ticket base, a corporate portal.

Four provider types are supported:

| Type | Notes |
|------|-------|
| **OpenID Connect** | Standard OIDC. Also the type that honours the email hint the shop passes when a customer has already typed their address. |
| **Azure AD B2C** | Microsoft's customer identity service, with its own policy setting. |
| **Laravel Passport** | OAuth via Laravel Passport. |
| **External** | Authentication handed over entirely to endpoints you host, rather than a protocol we speak. |

If your identity system speaks one of the first three, there is nothing to build — the platform speaks the protocol and you supply credentials. **External** is for owning the journey yourself; see [Acting as the Identity Provider](/integrate/identity/external-provider).

<Note>
Sending customers to a provider is a full-page journey, so an embedded shop lifts the customer out and returns them afterwards. To have the frame render already signed in, hand us the identity directly — see [Pre-authenticating a Session](/integrate/identity/pre-authentication).

Most delegated integrations use both: the provider for full-page visits, pre-authentication for frames.
</Note>

## Guest Checkout Works Alongside Any of These

Guest checkout is a company setting, not an alternative to the above. When it is enabled, a customer who reaches checkout without signing in can buy with just an email address, whichever of the three models is in place.

Guests receive a limited account: identified as **Guest Customer**, able to see only their most recent order, with no account area, profile editing or balance management. They are prompted to upgrade afterwards.

Where authentication is delegated, the shop presents its checkout sign-in card — **Sign in with \{provider name\}** alongside **Continue as guest** — rather than sending the customer straight out, so the guest option stays reachable.

An email address the customer has already typed is passed to the provider as a hint, so they return as the account they intended.

Upgrading a guest normally means setting a password. Where the shop holds no passwords, the upgrade routes through your provider instead:

> Sign in with \{provider name\} to get a full account, where you can track all your orders and view your payment history. We'll bring you straight back afterwards.

Signing in clears the guest flag and returns the customer to what they were doing.

See [Customer Accounts](/core-platform/ticket-shop/customer-accounts#guest-users) and [Checkout](/core-platform/ticket-shop/checkout#guest-checkout).

## Identifying the Same Person on Both Sides

Whichever model you choose, decide early **what identifies the customer**.

Matching on email address works until a customer changes theirs, at which point the next call looks like a different person: a second customer record appears and the original one keeps the order history.

The platform accepts an **external identifier** — your own stable identifier for the person — instead. It becomes the identity, and the email address becomes a mutable attribute: if the address we hold differs from the one you send, we update ours.

Supply it consistently from the first call. Both the [customer SSO API](/integrate/identity/pre-authentication) and the [basket](/integrate/checkout/building-baskets#saying-whose-basket-it-is) and [order](/integrate/checkout/api-checkout) endpoints accept it.

## Signing Out

Decide explicitly whether signing out of one system should end the session in the other.

| What you want | How to get it |
|---------------|---------------|
| Shop sign-out ends both sessions | Configure a logout URL for the company; the shop calls it after ending its own session |
| Provider sign-out ends both sessions | Send the customer to the shop's `/auth/end-session` endpoint |
| Each system independent | Configure no logout URL |
| No sign-out control inside an embedded frame | Add `hideLogout=1` to the frame URL |

Use `/auth/end-session` rather than the shop's normal sign-out when your provider starts the sign-out: it ends the shop session without calling your provider back. [Acting as the Identity Provider](/integrate/identity/external-provider#signing-out) covers both directions.

## Getting It Configured

Delegated authentication is configured at company level rather than from within the admin area, so it is arranged through your account manager — provider type, endpoints, credentials, and the claim your identifier arrives in. Have your provider details to hand.

Nothing on this page affects how your own staff sign in to the admin area. That is a separate feature with separate settings — see [Single Sign-On](/core-platform/system-access/single-sign-on).
